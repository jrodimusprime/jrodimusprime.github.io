The Algorithm Decided I'm a Foo Fighters Fan

I don't like or dislike Foo Fighters. I'm not into Nirvana. But my feeds kept pushing them, then pushed them harder, and now social media suggests Foo Fighters fan pages from my chats. It isn't fate, and it isn't the algorithm reading my mind. It's a well-studied flaw with a name and some simple maths behind it.

Jared Ryan · 4 November 2026

Researchers call it a degenerate feedback loop. The system makes a guess about you, shows you more of what it guessed, reads your reaction as proof, and guesses harder. It looks like the algorithm discovered your taste. Mostly, it manufactured the evidence.

How the loop forms

The maths of chicken and egg

The Pólya urn. Start with a jar holding one gold ball and one blue ball. Draw a ball, then put it back with another of the same colour. Early draws snowball. Run it many times and the jar settles into a mix decided mostly by luck in the first few draws, not by anything about you.

Explore versus exploit. A recommender has to choose between trying something new (exploring) and serving what seems to work (exploiting). Once it is confident that "rock fan" means Foo Fighters, it stops exploring, so it never learns you'd rather have something else.

Performative prediction. Perdomo and colleagues (2020) showed that when a prediction changes the behaviour it predicts, a system can settle on an answer that agrees with itself and is still wrong. The model doesn't just forecast your taste; it shapes it.

The evidence. Jiang and colleagues (2019) showed that without enough exploration these loops shrink what people get shown. Chaney, Stewart and Engelhardt (2018) found that this kind of feedback makes everyone's recommendations more alike and less useful to each of them.

It isn't discovering who you are. It's guessing, then nudging you until the guess looks right.

How to break it

  • Say no explicitly. "Don't recommend this artist", "Not interested", "Hide". An explicit no counts for far more than a skip.
  • Skip fast, or not at all. Listening for a while and then skipping can still count as a play.
  • Don't open the fan pages to dismiss them. Opening them is engagement.
  • Feed it what you do like. Play and save the specific bands you enjoy. That gives the model a different nearby favourite to latch onto.
  • Use the reset tools. Instagram lets you reset suggested content, YouTube lets you remove items from watch history, and Spotify lets you exclude a playlist from your taste profile.

The unsettling part isn't that the algorithm knows you. It's that, left alone, it will decide who you are from a few early accidents and keep proving itself right.
